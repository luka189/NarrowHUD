NarrowHUD Layout Editor

LAYOUT FILE v1 · NARROWHUD 2.0+

Drag the parts around the screen (arrow keys nudge the picked one; Ctrl+Z undoes), then copy the file into the game's folder. A screenshot of your game, picked, dropped or pasted, goes behind the parts.

How the editor works

The idea

A layout is a list of parts (the bar, the count, the magazines, the modes and their icons, stims, grenades, the backpack, health, stamina, the stim timer). Every part is either free (placed at a point on the screen) or pinned to another part (placed against it). Pinned parts are what make a layout hold together: hide a part and the one pinned to it takes its place; change Size and the group keeps its shape.

Free parts

A free part has an anchor (a point of the screen: the centre, an edge, a corner), x and y from that anchor in % of the screen's height (+ right, + up, so the same numbers work at every resolution), and an align: which point of the part sits on that spot. Drag it where you want it; the grid snaps the drop to 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.5 % (Alt for finer steps, Off for none).

Pinned parts

A pinned part has after (the part it follows), a pin (right of it, left of it, above or below), a gap in pixels between the two, and an align that says which edges line up: for right / left pins, bottoms, tops or middles; for above / below, left edges, right edges or centres. x and y are then small nudges from that spot. The gap and the nudges scale with Size, so the group looks the same at Small and Huge.

The quickest way to pin: drag a free part close to another part's edge with snap on — a green line shows the edge it will pin to, and the readout says "drop: pinned right of bar". Hold Ctrl while dragging to drop without pinning. Dragging a pinned part along its pin changes the gap; across it, the nudge. The arrow keys do the same in grid steps. "After: Nothing" in the fields unpins it, keeping its place.

To follow an edge cleanly (say, the stims under the bar's left end): pin below the bar with a *_left align and a gap of 8 — no x nudge. A gap and a nudge that cancel each other only look right at one Size.

A part pinned to a hidden part (off, or nothing to show: no backpack, a rifle with no charge meter) takes that part's place: the gap collapses to 0 — unless the hidden part holds its place: then its slot stays while it has nothing to show for the moment (the weapon put away, a stratagem ball in hand), and the parts pinned to it don't move. The ammo bar, the count, health and stamina hold by default (they only vanish for a moment); the parts a weapon may not have — the magazines, the charge meter, the modes, the backpack — give their place away. A loop of pins (a after b after a) can't be resolved; the editor says so and places the last one on its own.

Bars

The ammo bar, the charge meter, the backpack, health, stamina and the stim timer are bars: horizontal, vertical or curved (a "(" or a ")", with a radius), with a length and a thickness in pixels at 1440p Medium. Each can have its own edge (none, an outline, or the game's brackets) and shade (the faint empty part), or follow F9's choice (auto).

Arcs around the crosshair: give each curved bar the align Arc centre (the button under the align pad) with the centre anchor and x / y 0. The arc's own centre then sits on the crosshair, so arcs of different radii stay concentric at every Size — pinning their boxes to each other can't do that, since a box grows with its radius. Space them by radius: the next arc's outer edge 8 px inside the last one's inner edge (radius ± half the thickness), and keep the same length-to-radius ratio for the same sweep.

Sides and Spread

F9's Spread slider moves the parts left of the crosshair left and the parts right of it right. A part's side is auto (by where it sits), or set by hand; none keeps it still (health bars under the crosshair, say).

Seeing it

Pick a weapon sample to see how each kind looks (a magazine, a heat weapon, a charge weapon, a sniper with zeroing, every part at once), and a fill to see the bars at any level. Put a screenshot of your game behind the parts (pick, drop or paste one) and choose your screen's shape. Zoom in on the picked part for fine work (Space held or the middle button pans). The preview options show the look as F9 would set it (Size, edges, shade, Spread, colours). Which parts show when is F9's business (the Show rules), not the layout's: the layout says where.

Getting it into the game

  1. Copy the file (or Download it).
  2. In the game: F9 › NarrowHUD › Layout: Custom layout — NarrowHUD writes a layout file the first time.
  3. Replace that file's text: %APPDATA%\Arrowhead\Helldivers2\chef_data_luka189_2fnarrowhud__layout.lua (the one ending in __layout.lua; the __learned.lua next to it is NarrowHUD's own).
  4. Press the key set in F9 under Keys: reload layout file, or start a mission.

Copy a link to this layout puts the whole layout in a link: open it here and the layout is back, so you can share one or keep it as a bookmark. "Open a file…" or the paste box bring a file you have back in. The presets are starting points; Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo.

Keys

Arrow keys: nudge (Shift: 1 %). Shift+click or a drag-box: several parts at once. Ctrl while dragging: no snap. Alt while dragging: finer grid. Space or the middle button: pan when zoomed. Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y: undo / redo. Esc: close this.
